Approach

Defined by what is removed.

Extreme minimalism is the idea. It is how the buildings are chosen, and how the company stays close enough to the work to learn.

Take things out

Just Haus starts by asking what can leave. Fewer things to buy, handle, install, maintain, and replace. Assemblies that open. Choices made for this building.

Buildings are experiments

A project is a home and a test at the same time. Structure, air, interiors, energy, the order of the work: several questions run at once. It is like a dozen, on a busy day. What holds is what the next building gets to keep.

The next one inherits the lesson

Just Haus Alpha is the first building. What it teaches is meant to change whatever comes after. That is the system: repeated experiments, each one feeding the next.

Warm, on purpose

Minimal, and still warm. The architecture can stay quiet. Materials, daylight, seasons, and the life in the rooms do the rest. Wood that weathers is welcome. Lived-in is the goal.

Change it by opening it

Panels and surfaces come off, so the wall stays visible and the building stays whole. Repair and rearrangement belong in the design from the start.

A real winter

The place is the White Mountains. Snow, moisture, and a long heating season ask the questions. Energy is a set of tradeoffs, chosen for a real winter. Alpha will be measured, and the results shared here as they come in.

Technology that simplifies the day

Systems should make the building simpler to live with. A system earns its place when daily life gets simpler.

Every try stays in the record

Some lessons arrive sideways. Foam that winds up in the yard. A detail that met the weather and pointed to the next try. Those notes are evidence, and they stay on the page. The experiments page is where they belong, each test written as it happened.
