# Design checklist

On each project, mark every rule applied, an exception, or n/a, and write the reason; the why here is a draft to confirm, not a measured result.

## Doors and rooms

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Sliding doors wherever possible | Keeps the swing out of a small room, so the plan stays compact and easy to move through. |
| Privacy through mini hallways | A short transition between shared rooms and private ones lets a door close while the bedroom stays off the living room. |

## Windows and walls

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Keep the center of a wall clear | The middle stays free for furniture, a person, or a future opening. A window sits off that center. |
| Vertical windows on the east and west | East and west light arrives low. A tall, narrow opening meets it and leaves more solid wall. |
| Landscape windows on the south | South light lasts. A wide opening takes it in while the wall above and below stays useful. |
| A north window only when that wall is the room's only exterior wall | When another outside wall exists, light and view come from a warmer side. A north window is the room's one chance at the outdoors. |

## Roof and air

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| No roof penetrations | The roof stays one continuous weather surface. Vents, pipes, and skylights find another path. |
| No HVAC ducting. ERV airflow only | Fresh air is one intentional path through an ERV. |

## Approach and experiments

Already the practice on [Approach](../../approach.html) and [Experiments](../../journal.html).

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Subtract parts | Fewer things to buy, haul, fit, maintain, and replace. The plan can still change. |
| Removable panels where drywall would usually go | A panel comes off, so a change starts by opening the wall. |
| Assemblies that open | What comes apart can be checked, repaired, and rearranged. |
| A simpler foundation, with less basement and less concrete | Alpha is learning how little basement and concrete this climate asks for, and how the building rests on it. |
| Fresh air on purpose | A tight building takes fresh air as a decision, learned in use. See the ERV rule. |
| On-site power and storage, judged as a tradeoff | Power made on the building, stored, and spent in a cold climate is a tradeoff. Results are shared as they come in. |
| A build sequence that keeps the work visible | The order of work leaves each question visible until the wall is ready to close. |
| Warm minimalism, with natural materials | Quiet architecture. Wood that weathers, daylight, seasons, and the life in the rooms. Lived-in is the goal. |
