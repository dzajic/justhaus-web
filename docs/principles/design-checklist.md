# Design checklist

Use this on every project. For each rule, mark **applied**, **exception**, or **n/a**, and write a reason on the project page.

The short why under each rule is a **draft rationale for Daniel to confirm**. It is a starting reason, not a measured result. No performance numbers belong here until a project has them and they are ready to share.

## Doors and rooms

- [ ] **Sliding doors wherever possible.**
  - Why (draft): a sliding door keeps the swing out of a small room, so the plan can stay compact and still feel easy to move through.

- [ ] **Privacy through mini hallways.**
  - Why (draft): a small transition hall between the shared rooms and the private ones lets a door close without putting the bedroom on the living room. The separation is a few steps, not a long corridor.

## Windows and walls

- [ ] **Keep the center of a wall clear.**
  - Why (draft): the middle of a wall stays free for furniture, a person, or a future opening. A window belongs off that center.

- [ ] **Vertical windows on the east and west.**
  - Why (draft): east and west light arrives low. A tall, narrow opening meets that light and leaves more solid wall.

- [ ] **Landscape windows on the south.**
  - Why (draft): the south light lasts. A wide, horizontal opening takes it in while the wall above and below stays useful.

- [ ] **A north window only when that wall is the room's only exterior wall.**
  - Why (draft): when a room has another outside wall, it can take its light and view from a warmer side. A north window is there when it is the room's one chance at the outdoors.

## Roof and air

- [ ] **No roof penetrations.**
  - Why (draft): the roof stays one continuous weather surface. Vents, pipes, and skylights can find another path.

- [ ] **No HVAC ducting. ERV airflow only.**
  - Why (draft): fresh air is a single, intentional path through an ERV. A duct network is a lot of hidden length. The airflow rule is ERV only.

## From the public Approach and Experiments pages

These are already the practice on [Approach](../../approach.html) and [Experiments](../../journal.html). The why lines restate that public intent, still as draft rationale to confirm.

- [ ] **Subtract parts.**
  - Why (draft): fewer things to buy, haul, fit, maintain, and replace. Extreme minimalism is the idea, and the plan can still change.

- [ ] **Removable panels where drywall would usually go.**
  - Why (draft): a panel can come off, so a change starts by opening the wall. Use this where the work allows it.

- [ ] **Assemblies that open.**
  - Why (draft): if it can come apart, it can be checked, repaired, and rearranged. Repair belongs in the design from the start.

- [ ] **A simpler foundation, with less basement and less concrete.**
  - Why (draft): Alpha is asking how little basement and concrete this climate needs, and how well the building rests on it. The answer is still being learned.

- [ ] **Fresh air on purpose.**
  - Why (draft): a tight building asks for fresh air as a decision, learned in use. See the ERV rule above.

- [ ] **On-site power and storage, judged as a tradeoff.**
  - Why (draft): making power on the building, storing some of it, and spending it in a cold climate is a tradeoff. Alpha will be measured, and the results shared as they come in.

- [ ] **A build sequence that keeps the work visible.**
  - Why (draft): the order of the work should leave each question visible until the wall is ready to close.

- [ ] **Warm minimalism, with natural materials.**
  - Why (draft): the architecture can stay quiet. Wood that weathers, daylight, seasons, and the life in the rooms do the rest. Lived-in is the goal.
